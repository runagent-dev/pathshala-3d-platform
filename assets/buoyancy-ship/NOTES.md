# Notes: Archimedes' principle and buoyancy (আর্কিমিডিসের নীতি এবং প্লবতা)

Book: physics-9-10 › 5.3.1 › p137 (continues on p138)

## What the page shows
`reference/page-137.png`. A body held in a liquid seems to lose as much weight as the weight of the liquid it pushes
aside ("কোনো বস্তু তরলে নিমজ্জিত করলে সেটি যে পরিমাণ তরল অপসারণ করে সেইটুকু তরলের সমান ওজন বস্তুটির ওজন থেকে কমে
যায়"). চিত্র ৫.০৪: a cylinder of face area A and height h is held in a liquid of density ρ, its top face at depth h₁ and
its bottom face at depth h₂ ("চিত্র ৫.০৪: একটি বস্তু যতটুকু তরল অপসারিত করে তার সমপরিমাণ ওজন হারায়"). The pressure
on the top face P₁ = h₁ρg pushes down, on the bottom face P₂ = h₂ρg pushes up; F₁ = AP₁ = Ah₁ρg, F₂ = AP₂ = Ah₂ρg.
The forces on the sides cancel ("একদিক থেকে … ঠিক তার বিপরীত পরিমাণ বল অনুভব করে … কাটাকাটি করে দেয়"). Since h₂ > h₁,
F = F₂ − F₁ = A(h₂ − h₁)ρg = Ahρg; Ah is the cylinder's volume, so F is the weight of the liquid it pushed aside.
"ঊর্ধ্বমুখী এই বলটিকে প্লবতা (Buoyancy) বলে।"

## The one idea to teach
Water pushes harder on the bottom of a body than on its top (P = hρg), so it pushes it up with a force, প্লবতা, equal
to the weight of the water the body pushes aside (F = Ahρg = Vρg); a ship floats because that equals its weight.

## How the figure shows it (the owner's brief, 2026-10-31)
One cargo ship in a sunny harbour, the near end of the water cut away (a glass-like cut face just behind the
ship's flat stern) so the submerged hull and its arrows are visible. Four steps, one idea at a time (the row of words):
1. **চাপ**: pressure arrows on the hull, short near the waterline and long at the bottom (P = hρg); the arrows on the
   two sides are equal pairs and fade ("কাটাকাটি"); the bottom ones slide together into one bold প্লবতা arrow.
   F = P₂A = Ah₂ρg with the ship's numbers. The ship's top is out of the water, so h₁ = 0 and F₁ = 0 (said so).
2. **ওজন ও প্লবতা**: ওজন W = mg down, প্লবতা up, equal for the floating ship. A solid steel cylinder of the same
   mass as the empty ship (260 t) hangs on a spring balance beside it (the owner's spring-balance picture): lowered into
   the water the reading drops by its প্লবতা; let go, it sinks: the same weight, but it pushes aside only 33 m³ of
   water, so its প্লবতা is a seventh of its weight (the book's চিত্র ৫.০৪ is a cylinder too). A light touch of p138
   (5.3.2, ভেসে থাকা বা ডুবে যাওয়া): ঘনত্ব of steel 7,850 vs water 1,000 kg m⁻³.
3. **অপসারিত পানি**: a glowing "ghost" of the water the hull pushes aside (the submerged hull's shape, V = Ah) slides
   out from under the ship, rises out of the water and hangs on a spring balance (the owner's spring-balance picture):
   its weight Vρg equals the ship's প্লবতা. Marked "কল্পনা" (it is a thought experiment).
4. **মাল বোঝাই**: containers (20 t each, 0 to 12) are lowered onto the deck; the ship settles deeper with a small bob
   (the weight arrow grows first, the buoyancy catches up), the waterline climbs the draft marks on the stern and the
   load line (Plimsoll mark) on the side; at twelve containers the water reaches the load line.

The water's particles pushing on the hull (the owner's particle picture) are a few dots that drift up against the
bottom during step 1 only.

## Numbers (honest, round)
- Water ρ = 1,000 kg m⁻³ (fresh water: a river harbour; sea water would be about 1,025 and the book's page does not
  mention it, so it is left out), g = 9.8 m s⁻² (the book's).
- The hull below the deck is a prism (straight sides, flat bottom, pointed bow in plan): its waterplane area is
  A = 200 m² at every draft, so V = Ah exactly. Length 28 m, beam 8 m, depth 4 m.
- The ship with nothing on board: 260 t → h = 1.3 m; each container 20 t → +0.1 m; twelve → 500 t, h = 2.5 m (the
  load line). Buoyancy 25,48,000 N … 49,00,000 N. Average density 325 … 625 kg m⁻³ (mass over A × 4 m).
- The steel cylinder: 260 t of steel (ρ = 7,850 kg m⁻³), V ≈ 33.1 m³ (radius 1.6 m, height 4.12 m), প্লবতা ≈
  3,24,600 N (shown with "প্রায়").
- Arrows: forces on one scale, 0.7 m per 10 lakh N (the ship's and the cylinder's are comparable, so the cylinder's
  buoyancy arrow is honestly short); pressures on another, 0.57 m per 10,000 Pa.
