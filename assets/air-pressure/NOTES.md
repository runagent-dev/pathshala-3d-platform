# Notes: Air pressure (বাতাসের চাপ)

Book: physics-9-10 › 5.4 › p142–143 (`reference/page-142.png`; the owner's picture of a can before and after with
arrows pressing in from the sides: `reference/owner-can-crush.png`).

## What the page shows
- Air has a pressure (চিত্র ৫.০৬). We don't feel it because our body pushes out from inside too: the two cancel. In
  space there is no air, so nothing cancels the pressure inside, and astronauts wear pressure suits (চাপ নিরোধক স্পেস
  স্যুট).
- At the Earth's surface the pressure is 10⁵ N/m²: the column of air (বাতাসের স্তম্ভ) over 1 m² weighs 10⁵ N, roughly an
  elephant's weight. The picture is a tall see-through box on a 1 m × 1 m square.
- Weight is a force and acts downward; pressure is not a vector, it has no direction, so at any place it is the same
  in every direction (up, down, left, right, front, back). True for air and liquids.
- চিত্র ৫.০৭: a thin tin or aluminium box with a tube. When the air inside is pumped out, the outside air crushes it.
  (a) the box; (b) what would happen if pressure came only from the top: flattened; (c) what really happens: crushed
  from every side (সংকুচিত).

## The one idea to teach
The air presses on everything with 10⁵ N/m², from every side at once; we only notice it when the push back from
inside is taken away (the box crumples from all sides, not just from the top).

## What the student does
- **টিনের কৌটা** (the main part): taps the pump (or the big button) once per stroke. Each stroke takes 20,000 Pa out
  (made simple, said on the picture). Red arrows (outside air, 4 on each of the five open faces) stay the same; the
  blue arrows (inside air, seen through the tin) shrink; the box buckles in from every side and the camera leans in.
  The panel shows both pressures and works out F = (P outside − P inside) × A for one 10 cm × 10 cm face (A = 0.01
  m²): 200, 400 … 1,000 N. After five strokes: "যদি শুধু উপর থেকে চাপ দিত?" shows the box as a pale ghost flattening
  under arrows on the top only (marked as imagined), then "আসলে কী হয় দেখো" replays the crush from every side.
- **বাতাসের স্তম্ভ**: the 1 m × 1 m column fading upward (specks of air thinner higher up); a tap shows its weight
  10⁵ N as a purple arrow next to an elephant's (P = F/A = 10⁵ N/m²).
- **আমাদের শরীর**: a student with equal red arrows pressing in and blue arrows pushing out (they cancel); "মহাকাশে হলে
  কী হতো?" takes the yard away under the stars and leaves only the inside arrows (why the suit).
- Test: 5 questions (force on a face from the two pressures on the picture; what top-only pressure would do; why all
  sides; the column's weight over 1 m²; why we don't feel it).

## Choices made here (not in the book)
- The box is a cube 10 cm on a side so one face is a round 0.01 m²; the book's picture is a cube too.
- The pump takes equal 20,000 Pa steps (a real pump takes a fraction of what is left each stroke); said on the picture.
- The crumple grows with ΔP (a real can holds, then buckles suddenly); each stroke ends with a small spring, like a
  buckle.
- The space step shows a person under the stars without a suit, only as "what if", with the book's sentence.
- The elephant's weight is "about 10⁵ N" as the book says (a big elephant is 5–10 tonnes).

## Open questions
