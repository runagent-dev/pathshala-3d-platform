The everyday examples on the pages: wet clothes will not dry in the rainy season but dry in the shade indoors in winter;

## The one idea to teach
Evaporation (বাষ্পায়ন) happens at any temperature and is faster with more air flow, more surface, a more volatile
liquid, lower air pressure, more warmth and drier air. Same liquid, same amount: one thing different, and the vessel
with that thing runs dry sooner.

## What the figure does
Two identical vessels of the same amount of liquid; a row of the six words picks which thing is different (a fan; a
glass against a big plate, the book's own example; water against a volatile liquid; the right vessel under a bell jar
with the air pumped out; a warm plate; humid mist round the left vessel and dry air round the right). One big button
lets time pass; each vessel's share of liquid left is shown (a white ring marks the starting level), vapour rises in
proportion, and a time bar can be slid. It is a model (relative rates, arbitrary time units) and says nothing about
real seconds. The test mirrors the vessels at random.
