## The one idea to teach
A force is what starts a body at rest moving or changes a moving body's velocity; some forces act only by touching
(স্পর্শ বল: a crane's cable, a push, friction), others act without touching (অস্পর্শ বল: gravity on a falling
body, a magnet's pull).

## Terms (as in the book)
বল, স্থির বস্তু চলতে শুরু করে, গতিশীল বস্তুর বেগের পরিবর্তন, স্পর্শ বল, অস্পর্শ বল, ক্রেন দিয়ে ভারী জিনিস তোলা,
কোনো কিছুকে ধাক্কা দেওয়া, চলতে চলতে ঘর্ষণের জন্য চলন্ত বস্তুর থেমে যাওয়া, কোনো কিছু ছেড়ে দিলে মাধ্যাকর্ষণ বলের জন্য
নিচে পড়া, চুম্বকের আকর্ষণ. The panel quotes the book's definition word for word, and each example's own phrase from
the page. Other words the figure uses: ফাঁক (gap), ট্রলি, বেগ (velocity, as in 2.5), মি/সে.

## How the figure works
- Five chips (icon + short name) switch between the page's examples, in the page's order; each starts at rest:
  1. ক্রেন: a truck crane on its outriggers lifts a concrete block (1.5 m × 1 m × 1 m) by its steel ring, 1.3 m at up
     to 0.5 m/s (a real hoist speed), real time.
  2. ধাক্কা: a student in school uniform pushes a carton on a flat trolley (0 → 0.9 m/s in 0.9 s); then the hands
     let go and the trolley rolls on by itself, slowing a little. Real time.
  3. ঘর্ষণ: a wooden crate slides on a board floor at 2 m/s (it is already sliding before it is played: the book's
     "চলন্ত বস্তু"); friction (μ ≈ 0.2, so −1.96 m/s²) stops it in about 1 s and 1 m. Shown 2 × slower.
  4. মাধ্যাকর্ষণ: a student holds a football (22 cm) out with both hands, its bottom 1.04 m above the floor, and lets
     go: it falls at g (4.5 m/s at the floor), bounces a little and stops. Shown 3 × slower.
  5. চুম্বক: a bar magnet (6 cm, N red, S blue) is brought from 6 cm to 3 cm from an iron nail (5 cm) on a table; the
     nail then rushes the last 3 cm to the N pole, faster and faster (about 0.12 s in reality). Shown 10 × slower.
- চালাও (or a tap on the example; the hand of the first-use pointer shows where): the force acts. Its orange arrow
  appears where it acts, with its name (ক্রেনের টান, হাতের ধাক্কা, মেঝের ঘর্ষণ, মাধ্যাকর্ষণ বল, চুম্বকের আকর্ষণ),
  and where the force meets the body is marked: a blue ring on the touching place (hook in the ring, the hands on the
  carton), a thick blue line along the crate's bottom edge, or a dashed violet bracket across the empty gap (below the
  falling ball, between the nail's tip and the magnet).
- The arrow is shown only while the force acts (physics): the crane keeps holding the block (arrow stays); the push
  ends when the hands leave the trolley; sliding friction ends when the crate stops; gravity and the magnet keep
  pulling after the ball lands and the nail sticks (arrow stays, the gap bracket goes).
- The first time, each example stops at its telling moment (block half a metre up; hands pushing; crate still
  sliding; ball in the air; nail on its way with a gap). The panel asks "এই বল প্রয়োগ করতে কি স্পর্শ করতে হয়?" and the
  two boxes (স্পর্শ বল, solid blue; অস্পর্শ বল, dashed violet) pulse. A wrong box is refused with a pointer to what to
  look at ("আবার দেখো: চুম্বক আর পেরেকের মাঝে ফাঁক, কিছুই ছুঁয়ে নেই।"); the right one puts the example's icon in the box,
  labels the marker (স্পর্শ / ফাঁক: স্পর্শ নেই), and the action goes on to the end. The next unsorted chip glows.
- The book's definition is always in the panel; once an example acts, the part it shows is highlighted: "স্থির বস্তু
  চলতে শুরু করে" (crane, push, magnet), "গতিশীল বস্তুর বেগের পরিবর্তন হয়" (friction), both (the ball). A live readout
  shows the velocity in m/s (real values, also when shown slower). Counter: বাছাই n/5 → সব বাছাই হলো!
- Keys: 1–5 or ←/→ choose the example, space/Enter/P play, C and N sort (স্পর্শ / অস্পর্শ), F/V views.
- Test (5 questions, order shuffled), each with a worked answer that leads with the answer:
  1. a new contact case in 3D: a student pulls a cart with a rope (the student never touches the cart) or a
     parachute (the air, unseen, touches the canopy); answers pair a kind with a reason, the wrong ones from real
     mistakes ("the person does not touch the cart", "air cannot be seen", "gravity slows it", "no force");
  2. a new non-contact case: a comb rubbed on hair lifts bits of paper (wrong: the hand holds the comb, the paper
     ends up stuck to the comb, "no force: the paper is light");
  3. what the force is doing (the crate's friction, the falling ball's gravity or the magnet's pull, stopped in the
     middle): faster / slower / same / nothing; the answer then plays out;
  4. the first law as evidence: what tells for sure that a force acts (velocity changing, not "it keeps moving", "it
     stays at rest", "it is heavy"), over an example that plays from rest;
  5. which of the book's examples is the same kind as the magnet (or as the falling ball).

## Open questions
- The Earth pulling the Moon (suggested for the test) is left to the gravitational-force figure (3.2.1), so the test
  uses the comb as its new non-contact case. Kicking a football was left out as too close to the push.
- The crane's arrow is drawn beside the cable (not on it) so the hook stays visible; the ball's weight arrow is drawn
  from its middle, over the ball.
- The magnet is moved in with no hand drawn (a hand would add a second, unrelated contact force). The panel says
  "চুম্বক কাছে আনতেই".
