## The one idea to teach
A force is what starts a body at rest moving or changes a moving body's velocity; some forces act only by touching
(স্পর্শ বল: a crane's cable, a push, friction), others act without touching (অস্পর্শ বল: gravity on a falling
body, a magnet's pull).

## Terms (as in the book)
বল, স্থির বস্তু চলতে শুরু করে, গতিশীল বস্তুর বেগের পরিবর্তন, স্পর্শ বল, অস্পর্শ বল, ক্রেন দিয়ে ভারী জিনিস তোলা,
কোনো কিছুকে ধাক্কা দেওয়া, চলতে চলতে ঘর্ষণের জন্য চলন্ত বস্তুর থেমে যাওয়া, কোনো কিছু ছেড়ে দিলে মাধ্যাকর্ষণ বলের জন্য
নিচে পড়া, চুম্বকের আকর্ষণ. The panel quotes the book's definition word for word, and each example's own phrase from
the page. Other words the figure uses: ফাঁক (gap), ট্রলি, বেগ (velocity, as in 2.5), মি/সে. The ball is always called
ফুটবল: on this page বল means force ("স্থির বল পড়তে শুরু করল" would read as "a force started to fall").

## How the figure works
- Five chips (icon + short name) switch between the page's examples, in the page's order. Each view is framed on the
  moving body and its path (on a phone the crane's cab, the student's head and the magnet's S end run off the edges):
  1. ক্রেন: a truck crane on its outriggers lifts a concrete block (1.5 m × 1 m × 1 m) by its steel ring, 1.3 m at up
     to 0.5 m/s (a real hoist speed), real time. A soft blob shadow under the block shows the lift.
  2. ধাক্কা: a student in school uniform pushes a carton on a flat trolley (0 → 0.9 m/s in 0.9 s); then the hands
     let go and the trolley rolls on by itself while floor friction slowly stops it. Real time.
  3. ঘর্ষণ: a wooden crate slides on a board floor at 2 m/s (already sliding in the first picture, with its friction
     arrow: the book's "চলন্ত বস্তু"); friction (μ ≈ 0.2, so −1.96 m/s²) stops it in about 1 s and 1 m. Shown 2 ×
     slower.
  4. মাধ্যাকর্ষণ: a student holds a football (22 cm) out with both hands, its bottom 1.04 m above the floor, and lets
     go: it falls at g (4.5 m/s at the floor), bounces a little and stops. Shown 3 × slower. Its weight arrow is there
     from the first picture (the hands hold it up), in the air, and on the floor (the floor holds it up).
  5. চুম্বক: a hand brings a bar magnet (6 cm, N red, S blue) from 5 cm to 3 cm from an iron nail (5 cm) on a plain
     table; the nail then rushes the last 3 cm to the N pole, faster and faster (about 0.12 s in reality; shown 10 ×
     slower). The pull arrow grows as the gap closes. The hand touches the magnet, nothing touches the nail.
- চালাও (or a tap on the example; the first-use hand says "চেপে চালাও"): the force acts. While it is being set up (the
  cable tightening, the hands reaching the carton or letting go, the magnet brought near) the panel says so and the
  body is still; once the body moves, its orange arrow appears where the force acts, with its name (ক্রেনের টান, হাতের
  ধাক্কা, মেঝের ঘর্ষণ, মাধ্যাকর্ষণ বল, চুম্বকের আকর্ষণ), and where the force meets the body is marked: a hollow blue
  ring round the touching place (the hook in the ring, the hands on the carton: both stay visible inside it), a thick
  blue line along the crate's bottom edge, or a dashed violet bracket across the empty gap.
- The arrow is shown exactly while the force acts: the crane keeps holding the block, gravity and the magnet keep
  pulling (arrows stay; the end line says what holds the body: the pull equals the weight, the floor, the magnet's
  side); the push ends when the hands leave the trolley; sliding friction ends when the crate stops.
- The first time, each example stops at its telling moment (block half a metre up; hands pushing; crate still
  sliding; football in the air; nail on its way with a gap). The panel asks "এই বল প্রয়োগ করতে কি স্পর্শ করতে হয়?" and
  the two boxes (স্পর্শ বল, solid blue; অস্পর্শ বল, dashed violet) pulse. A wrong box is refused with what to look at
  ("আবার দেখো: চুম্বক আর পেরেকের মাঝে ফাঁক, কিছুই ছুঁয়ে নেই।"); the right one puts the example's icon in the box, labels
  the marker (স্পর্শ / ফাঁক: স্পর্শ নেই), and the action goes on to the end. The next unsorted chip glows.
- The book's definition is always in the panel; once the body moves, the part it shows is highlighted: "স্থির বস্তু
  চলতে শুরু করে" (crane, push, magnet), "গতিশীল বস্তুর বেগের পরিবর্তন হয়" (friction), both (the football). A live
  readout shows the velocity: its size in m/s (real values, also when shown slower) and an arrow for its direction
  (up, down, right; the football's turns at a bounce). Counter: বাছাই n/5 → সব বাছাই হলো!
- Keys: 1–5 or ←/→ choose the example, space/Enter/P play, C and N sort (স্পর্শ / অস্পর্শ), F/V views.
- Test (5 questions, order shuffled, never the same book example twice), each with a worked answer that leads with
  the answer and, after a wrong tap, speaks to that very mistake:
  1. a new contact case in 3D: a student pulls a cart with a rope (the student never touches the cart) or a
     parachute (the air, unseen, touches the canopy); the arrow is labelled only "এই বল" until the answer (its name
     would give the reason away); answers pair a kind with a reason, the wrong ones from real mistakes ("the person
     does not touch the cart", "air cannot be seen", "gravity slows it down", "no force");
  2. a new non-contact case: a comb rubbed on hair lifts bits of paper (wrong: the hand holds the comb, the paper
     ends up stuck to the comb, the right kind for a wrong reason "the paper is light");
  3. what the force is doing (the crate's friction, the football's gravity or the magnet's pull, stopped in the
     middle): faster / slower / same / nothing; the answer then plays out;
  4. the first law as evidence: what tells for sure that a force acts (velocity changing, not "it keeps moving", "it
     stays at rest", "it is moving very fast"), over an example that plays from rest;
  5. which of the book's examples is the same kind as the magnet (or as the falling football).

## Open questions
- The Earth pulling the Moon (suggested for the test) is left to the gravitational-force figure (3.2.1), so the test
  uses the comb as its new non-contact case. Kicking a football was left out as too close to the push.
- The crane's arrow is drawn beside the cable (not on it) so the hook stays visible; the football's weight arrow is
  drawn from its middle, over the ball.
- The football's bounce uses a restitution of 0.45 (a softly pumped ball): a well-pumped one (about 0.7) would bounce
  for twice as long, and the example is about the fall.
- The magnet's pull is drawn once the nail starts to move; while the magnet is still far, the pull is too weak to
  overcome the table's friction (not shown, to keep "a force starts a body at rest moving" clear).
