# Notes: A water barometer (পানির ব্যারোমিটার)

Book: physics-9-10 › 5.4.1 › p145. Screenshot: `reference/page-145.png` (both pages). The owner's picture:
`reference/owner-barometer.png`. Split from `torricelli-barometer` (1.0.0), where it was the fifth of six scenes.

## What the page shows
A 10.5 m straw would lift a cold drink only to 10.3 m however hard you suck (a cold drink is about as dense as
water). A mercury straw stops at 76 cm, because mercury is 13.6 times as dense as water (পানির ঘনত্ব থেকে পারদের
ঘনত্ব 13.6 গুণ বেশি).

## The one idea to teach
The same air's pressure holds up 76 cm of mercury or 10.3 m of water: the lighter the liquid, the taller the column.

## What the student does
One object, one button, one idea (the owner: "too much; should be easier"). In a schoolyard a tall glass pipe stands
in a tub of water against a three-storey school (for the scale), beside a small 76 cm mercury barometer and a
measuring staff. পারদের বদলে পানি নাও fills it the Torricelli way: the water rises to 10.3 m, nearly to the roof (the
camera follows it up), and stops with a vacuum above. One outlined air-pressure arrow on the tub. The panel: one short
line, the water's and the mercury's columns, the rule (76 cm × 13.6 ≈ 10.3 m). Test: 3 questions (how high, why so
high, what is above it).

## Terms (as in the book)
কোল্ড ড্রিংকস, 10.3 মিটার, পারদ, ঘনত্ব, "13.6 গুণ", পারদ স্তম্ভ, শূন্যতা, বাতাসের চাপ.

## Look
A brick schoolyard under a blue sky; the school is plain (no trees, no crowds). The pipe is drawn thicker than a real
one would need to be.

## Open questions
- The book writes 10.3 m with Latin digits; the figure follows the project rule (Bangla digits).
