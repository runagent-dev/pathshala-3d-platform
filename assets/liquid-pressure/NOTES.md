## What the page shows
Topic ৫.৩ তরলের ভেতর চাপ (Pressure in Liquids). Anyone who has swum knows you feel a pressure deep in water (we don't
feel the air's, because our body pushes back as much). The pressure at a depth comes from the weight of the column of
liquid above: "তোমার উপরে তরলের যে স্তম্ভটুকু থাকবে তার ওজন থেকেই তোমার উপরের চাপ নির্ণয় করতে হবে।" Imagine a surface of
area A at the depth h (চিত্র 5.02: a beaker with a dashed column above a surface A, the height h marked; caption "তরলের
উচ্চতার জন্য নিচের পৃষ্ঠে চাপ সৃষ্টি হয়।"). The liquid above A has the volume Ah; with density ρ its weight or force is
**F = mg = (Ahρ)g**, so **P = F/A = Ahρg/A = hρg**. "অর্থাৎ নির্দিষ্ট ঘনত্বের তরলে গভীরতার সাথে সাথে চাপ বাড়তে থাকে। পানির
বেলায় আনুমানিক প্রতি দশ মিটার গভীরতায় বাতাসের চাপের সমপরিমাণ চাপ বেড়ে যায়। ভূপৃষ্ঠে বায়ুমণ্ডলের চাপকে 1 atm বলা হয়, যার
পরিমাণ হলো 101325 Pa!" Page 135: a liquid cannot be squeezed by pressure the way a gas can, so its density stays the
same and the pressure grows at a steady rate; চিত্র 5.03 is a straight-line graph of পানির চাপ (atm) against গভীরতা (km),
from the sea's surface to 6 km.

The next sections are separate figures: 5.3.1 আর্কিমিডিসের নীতি এবং প্লবতা (p137: pressure acts in every direction, the
net upward force on a cylinder, buoyancy), 5.3.2 বস্তুর ভেসে থাকা বা ডুবে যাওয়া (p138), 5.3.3 প্যাসকেলের সূত্র (p141).

## The one idea to teach
The pressure at a depth h in a liquid is the weight of the column of liquid above, per area: P = hρg. Deeper means more
pressure, at a steady rate (twice the depth, twice the pressure), seen as stronger jets from deeper holes.

## Design
- A courtyard at sunset: terracotta floor tiles, a saffron lime-plastered wall with a band of cobalt and white tiles and
  a marigold garland, an evening sky (no green, no white). A clear glass tank, water 1 m deep in a rich blue that darkens
  towards the bottom, on a brick stand 0.5 m high; a stone channel on the floor catches the jets.
- Three brass spouts at 0.2, 0.4 and 0.6 m below the surface (1,960, 3,920, 5,880 Pa: 1 : 2 : 3), each with a cork. The
  jets follow Torricelli's parabola to scale (v = √(2gh), landing 2√(h·y) away): with the tank on its stand the deeper
  the hole, the further the jet, as in the owner's picture. The book does not do this sum; the figure only shows it.
  The level stays at 1 m (as if topped up): the picture is of the moment the holes are open.
- Inside the water, the book's figure 5.02: a dashed column above a teal surface A = 100 cm² (a disc) at the depth h, a
  purple arrow for its weight F (length in proportion to F, i.e. to h), a blue dimension h. Colours as `pressure`
  (book-notes): P red, F purple, A teal; h blue as in chapter 4.
- At each open hole a bold red arrow in a white outline pushes towards the spout, **length ∝ P** (15 cm for 6,000 Pa).
  A depth gauge beside the glass, 0 at the surface; once two holes are open it fills with red, pale at the top and deep
  at the bottom, with the words "গভীরতা বাড়লে চাপ বাড়ে" (the owner's picture's arrow, shown only after the student has
  compared two holes so the start doesn't give it away).
- One action, one result, one rule: the row of words is the three holes (each shows its P once open); ① ছিদ্রটি খোলো →
  ② ③ পরের ছিদ্র খোলো → ছিদ্রগুলো বন্ধ করো. A tap on a spout opens it too. The first opening takes the camera close to the
  hole (the arrow, A, the column, F) and back; a quiet button toggles the close view. A can be dragged up and down
  (or the arrow keys, 5 cm a step). The result line is P = hρg with the numbers and the book's units; the rule grows:
  the column's weight (F = (Ahρ)g with the number) → pressure grows with depth → about 1 atm per ten metres of water
  (10 m: 98,000 Pa; 1 atm = 101325 Pa as the book prints it). On tall phones (and 1280×800) the panel also shows the book's
  graph (5.03) for this tank: P against h, a straight line, a dot for A and one for each open hole.
- Test: P at a marked hole with its depth read off the gauge (mistakes: g left out, cm, another hole); why the bottom
  jet goes furthest (column tallest; denser water low down, the book says it isn't; bigger g; more air pressure); A
  lowered to twice the depth (দ্বিগুণ; অর্ধেক, একই, চার গুণ); what makes the pressure on A with the holes closed (the column's
  weight; the wall; the water below; no pressure); the pressure 10 m down (98,000; 9,800; 10,000; 9,80,000 Pa).

## Open questions
- The jets (Torricelli) and the corks are the owner's picture, not the book's; the panel never gives their speed or reach.
- P here is the water's own pressure (hρg), without the air's 1 atm on top, as the page's formula has it.
- The graph is the book's 5.03 drawn for this 1 m tank in Pa, not for the sea in km and atm.
- The pressure arrows point at the spouts (sideways): the page says nothing about direction; 5.3.1 does ("সব দিকে").
