## The one idea to teach

Energy is the capacity to do work: positive work gives an object energy, negative work takes it away, and in between
energy only changes form (here from height to motion), its total staying the same when nothing takes it away.

## How the figure shows it

The owner's reference picture (a cyclist going up a hill, "Energy in", "Potential energy" at the top, "Energy out"
going down) as a real 3D scene, with the book's words instead of the picture's English labels:

1. **ধনাত্মক কাজ** (step ১, "প্যাডেল করে পাহাড়ে ওঠো"): the rider pedals up at 3 m/s; an orange arrow along the road shows
   the pedal force along the motion; the energy bar grows as the work is done. The rule quotes the book's line and
   says the rider has to give that energy, from the chemical energy in their body ("যে বল প্রয়োগ করছে তাকে ঠিক সেই পরিমাণ
   শক্তি দিতে হয়"; রাসায়নিক শক্তি is on the page).
2. At the top it is at rest, yet it has 3,430 J: stored by its height (বিভব শক্তি), "এই শক্তি দিয়ে সাইকেলটি কাজ করতে পারে".
3. **শক্তির রূপান্তর** (step ২, "চূড়া থেকে গড়িয়ে নামতে দাও"): no pedalling, no friction; the purple part (বিভব শক্তি) turns
   into the amber part (গতিশক্তি), the bar's length stays 3,430 J. On the flat road it rolls on at about 9.9 m/s.
4. **ঋণাত্মক কাজ** (step ৩, "ব্রেক করো"): a crimson arrow against the motion, the brake pads glow, and the energy leaves
   the bicycle as heat in the brakes (hatched part of the bar): "যে বল প্রয়োগ করেছে সে এই শক্তিটুকু কোনো না কোনোভাবে
   পেয়ে যায়". The last rule gives the scalar and the unit J (জুল).

Numbers (the page has none, so these are chosen and realistic): rider and bicycle 70 kg, g = 9.8 m/s², hill 5 m high
→ 70 × 9.8 × 5 = 3,430 J; at the bottom v = √(2gh) ≈ 9.9 m/s (36 km/h); climbing at 3 m/s (½ × 70 × 3² ≈ 320 J of
kinetic energy while climbing); braking at 4 m/s². The bar's values come from mgh and ½mv² (4.3.1 and 4.3.2 later in
the chapter); the figure shows only the joules, not the formulas, because page 102 has neither.

Colours: বিভব শক্তি purple (#6741d9, gravity's colour in the chapter-3 figures), গতিশক্তি amber (as book-notes), heat
hatched grey, pedal force orange (a pull, #e8590c), brake force crimson (a friction, #c2255c). Every colour also has a
word next to it.

Stylised on purpose (said on the view: "ঘর্ষণ ও বাতাসের বাধা ধরা হয়নি"): no friction or air resistance, so the total on the
way down is exactly the same. The hill is steeper than a real road (about 26° at its steepest) so the whole climb fits
on a phone.

রূপান্তর, গতিশক্তি, রাসায়নিক শক্তি, তাপশক্তি (the figure says "ব্রেকে তাপ"), স্কেলার, জুল (J), মাত্রা ML²T⁻².

## Open questions for the book team

- বিভব শক্তি is defined only on page 106 (4.3.2), and with a spring, not height; the figure uses the word on page 102
  for the energy stored by height, explained plainly ("উঁচুতে থাকার জন্য জমা"). Is that acceptable here, or should the
  bar say "উচ্চতার জন্য শক্তি" until 4.3.2?
- The numbers (70 kg, 5 m) are the figure's own; the page has no example.
- The rider's work going into the bicycle "from the chemical energy in the body" follows the page's sentence about the
  one applying the force giving the energy; strictly, the road's static friction does the work on the bicycle as a
  whole. Fine for class 9?
