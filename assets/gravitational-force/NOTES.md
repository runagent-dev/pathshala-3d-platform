# Notes: Gravitational force (মহাকর্ষ বল)

Book: physics-9-10 › 3.2.1 › p68 (the first of four fundamental-force figures: 3.2.1–3.2.4, `order: 1`)

## What the page shows
Page 68 (`reference/page-68.png`, the first section) has no picture for 3.2.1, only two paragraphs:

> ৩.২.১ মহাকর্ষ বল (Gravitational Force): মহাবিশ্বের সকল বস্তু তাদের ভরের কারণে একে অপরকে যে বল দিয়ে আকর্ষণ করে,
> সেটিই হচ্ছে মহাকর্ষ বল। এই মহাকর্ষ বলের কারণে গ্যালাক্সির ভেতরে নক্ষত্ররা ঘুরপাক খায়, সূর্যকে ঘিরে পৃথিবী ঘোরে,
> কিংবা পৃথিবীকে ঘিরে চাঁদ ঘোরে। পৃথিবীর মহাকর্ষ বল যখন আমাদের ওপর কাজ করে, এই মহাকর্ষণ বলকে বলা হয় মাধ্যাকর্ষণ
> বল। এই মাধ্যাকর্ষণ বল আমাদের পৃথিবীর কেন্দ্রের দিকে, অর্থাৎ নিচের দিকে টেনে রেখেছে। এর কারণেই আমরা নিজেদের
> ওজনের অনুভূতি পাই।
> পদার্থবিজ্ঞানের একটি চমকপ্রদ বল হচ্ছে মহাকর্ষ বল। ভর আছে সেরকম যেকোনো বস্তু অন্য বস্তুকে মহাকর্ষ বল দিয়ে আকর্ষণ
> করে। মহাকর্ষ বলের পাল্লা অসীম।

## The one idea to teach
Every body with mass attracts every other body (মহাকর্ষ বল), from two apples to the stars of a galaxy, with no limit to
its reach; the Earth's pull on us (মাধ্যাকর্ষণ বল) points to the Earth's centre: that is what "down" means, and why
we feel our weight.

## Terms (as in the book)
মহাকর্ষ বল, ভর, আকর্ষণ (করে), একে অপরকে, গ্যালাক্সি, নক্ষত্র, ঘুরপাক খায়, সূর্য, পৃথিবী, চাঁদ, মাধ্যাকর্ষণ বল,
পৃথিবীর কেন্দ্র, নিচের দিকে, ওজনের অনুভূতি, পাল্লা, অসীম. This page writes ওপর (not উপর), so the figure does too.
From 3.1 (p64): নিউটনের প্রথম সূত্র, সমবেগে, সরলরেখায়.

## How the figure works
Five stops (chips in the panel), from the smallest distance to the largest; each pair is drawn with two equal violet
arrows (মহাকর্ষ বল, the colour the four force figures share), each stop says its distance:
- **আপেল**: two 200 g apples 20 cm apart on a table. The student drags the right one away (20–40 cm); the arrows
  (drawn many times bigger, in proportion to the pull) shrink as 1/d² but never vanish, and the panel shows the pull
  from Newton's law: about 6.7 × 10⁻¹¹ N at 20 cm. Once the apple has been moved, the panel compares it with the
  Earth's pull on each apple (about 2 N: about 2,900 crore times more at 20 cm, 12,000 crore at 40 cm). "সকল বস্তু"
  made real, and the pull is far too small to move them.
- **পৃথিবী** (the start): a globe (north up, longitude 0° facing the viewer, Bangladesh marked in the flag's green with
  a red dot at Dhaka) with four people on its edge, each holding up an apple: you in Bangladesh (a sky-blue school
  shirt), people at both poles, and one exactly opposite Bangladesh, in a boat on the Pacific. Each has a violet arrow
  from the middle of the body towards the centre and a dashed line on to the centre: they all meet there. The student
  drags themself anywhere round the circle (on land, or in a boat at sea; the panel names the place; anyone you come
  within 14° of steps aside), and taps "আপেল ছাড়ো": every apple falls at its person's feet, towards the centre
  (0.45 s from about a metre, shown twice as slow, the note says so), leaving faint copies at a quarter, a half and
  three quarters of the time (1/16, 1/4, 9/16 of the way: it speeds up). The apples stay down until something changes,
  so the still picture shows four falls, all towards the centre (the South Pole's "up" the screen). The people stand on
  the circle through both poles along 90° E / 90° W, the edge of the globe in the front view, so they are always seen
  side-on.
- **চাঁদ** and **সূর্য**: the Moon round the Earth, the Earth round the Sun (lit by the Sun); the Earth is tilted so its
  middle latitudes and Asia face the camera above. "টান না থাকলে?" takes the pull away: the camera goes back about
  twice as far (the circle gets small), the body leaves its circle along the tangent, in a straight line at the same
  speed (Newton's first law, page 64), for about 3 s (the Earth 4–5 s) until it reaches the edge of the picture (or the
  corner buttons or the note); there the dashed path ends in an arrowhead with "সরলরেখায় চলতেই থাকবে", and it stays
  gone until the student taps "টান ফিরিয়ে দাও".
- **গ্যালাক্সি**: a spiral galaxy drawn as dots (a printed-diagram look on the white page), its arms trailing behind the
  turning disc as real arms do, our Sun marked about half-way out on an arm with its pull towards the centre.
  "পাল্লা অসীম".

A strip at the foot of the learn panel shows the four fundamental forces weakest to strongest with their ranges
(অসীম, 10⁻¹⁸ m, অসীম, 10⁻¹⁵ m), মহাকর্ষ highlighted (left out at 390×520, where the 3D view needs the room).

Honest notes in the corner: people and apples drawn much bigger, the fall twice as slow; force arrows drawn many times
bigger; sizes, distances and time not to scale; the galaxy's Sun drawn bigger and time much faster. The galaxy turns as
a whole (stylised: real galaxies turn faster near the middle).

The test (5 questions, 4 taps each, in a new order every time, the range last so no later question can be answered
from its picture; each scene set up for its question, the pulls hidden when they would give the answer away; the view
cannot be turned during the test; a worked answer that names the student's mistake):
- the direction of the pull (or of a dropped apple) for you standing somewhere on the Earth, answered with four arrows
  (towards the centre, away, along the ground, down the screen);
- what keeps the Moon or the Earth going round, or where the Moon would go if the pull vanished (the answer shows the
  flight);
- which pull is called মাধ্যাকর্ষণ বল ("পৃথিবী আমাদের যে বলে টানে", the book's words), or, from the picture, whether the
  person at the South Pole (upside down in the picture) feels their weight; the answer drops all the apples;
- do two apples attract each other;
- the range (the galaxy), or the pull if the Moon were twice as far (a quarter, never zero).

## Numbers used
- G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²; apples 0.2 kg, 7 cm across, centres 20–40 cm apart: 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N … 1.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N;
  g = 9.8 m/s², so the Earth pulls each apple with about 2 N.
- The Earth's radius about 6,400 km (6,371 km); the Moon about 3 lakh 84 thousand km away, one turn in about 27 days
  (27.3); the Sun about 15 crore km away (14.96), one turn a year; the Sun about 26 thousand light-years from the
  galaxy's centre, one turn in about 23 crore years (about 230 million).
- In the figure: the Moon turns once in 10 s, the Earth round the Sun in 14 s, the galaxy in 48 s.

## Open questions
- The book's strengths conflict (3.2.2: electromagnetic is 10³⁶ × gravity; 3.2.4: strong is 10⁴¹ × gravity and 100 ×
  electromagnetic, which would make it 10³⁸ × gravity). The strip therefore gives only the order, weakest to
  strongest, and no numbers. For the book team.
- মাধ্যাকর্ষণ বল: the book defines it as the Earth's gravitational force acting on us. The test calls only the
  Earth's pull on us মাধ্যাকর্ষণ (the book's words) and avoids the Earth's pull on the Moon, which some books also call
  মাধ্যাকর্ষণ (or অভিকর্ষ).
- The fourth chip is called "পৃথিবী" (people on the Earth); "আমরা" was the other choice.
- The strip's heading "চার মৌলিক বল (সবচেয়ে দুর্বল → সবচেয়ে শক্তিশালী)" is the one all four force figures use (the page
  itself says "চার ধরনের বল" on p69).
