# Notes: Gravitational force (মহাকর্ষ বল)

Book: physics-9-10 › 3.2.1 › p68 (the first of four fundamental-force figures: 3.2.1–3.2.4, `order: 1`)

## What the page shows
Page 68 (`reference/page-68.png`, the first section) has no picture for 3.2.1, only two paragraphs:

> ৩.২.১ মহাকর্ষ বল (Gravitational Force): মহাবিশ্বের সকল বস্তু তাদের ভরের কারণে একে অপরকে যে বল দিয়ে আকর্ষণ করে,
> সেটিই হচ্ছে মহাকর্ষ বল। এই মহাকর্ষ বলের কারণে গ্যালাক্সির ভেতরে নক্ষত্ররা ঘুরপাক খায়, সূর্যকে ঘিরে পৃথিবী ঘোরে,
> কিংবা পৃথিবীকে ঘিরে চাঁদ ঘোরে। পৃথিবীর মহাকর্ষ বল যখন আমাদের ওপর কাজ করে, এই মহাকর্ষণ বলকে বলা হয় মাধ্যাকর্ষণ
> বল। এই মাধ্যাকর্ষণ বল আমাদের পৃথিবীর কেন্দ্রের দিকে, অর্থাৎ নিচের দিকে টেনে রেখেছে। এর কারণেই আমরা নিজেদের
> ওজনের অনুভূতি পাই।
> পদার্থবিজ্ঞানের একটি চমকপ্রদ বল হচ্ছে মহাকর্ষ বল। ভর আছে সেরকম যেকোনো বস্তু অন্য বস্তুকে মহাকর্ষ বল দিয়ে আকর্ষণ
> করে। মহাকর্ষ বলের পাল্লা অসীম।

## The one idea to teach
Every body with mass attracts every other body (মহাকর্ষ বল), from two apples to the stars of a galaxy, with no limit to
its reach; the Earth's pull on us (মাধ্যাকর্ষণ বল) points to the Earth's centre: that is what "down" means, and why
we feel our weight.

## Terms (as in the book)
মহাকর্ষ বল, ভর, আকর্ষণ (করে), একে অপরকে, গ্যালাক্সি, নক্ষত্র, ঘুরপাক খায়, সূর্য, পৃথিবী, চাঁদ, মাধ্যাকর্ষণ বল,
পৃথিবীর কেন্দ্র, নিচের দিকে, ওজনের অনুভূতি, পাল্লা, অসীম. This page writes ওপর (not উপর), so the figure does too.
From 3.1 (p64): নিউটনের প্রথম সূত্র, সমবেগে, সরলরেখায়.

## How the figure works
Five stops (chips in the panel), from the smallest distance to the largest; each pair is drawn with two equal violet
arrows (মহাকর্ষ বল, the colour the four force figures share), each stop says its distance:
- **আপেল**: two 200 g apples 20 cm apart on a table. The student drags the right one away (20–40 cm); the arrows
  (drawn many times bigger, in proportion to the pull) shrink as 1/d² but never vanish, and the panel shows the pull
  from Newton's law: about 6.7 × 10⁻¹¹ N at 20 cm. "সকল বস্তু" made real, and the pull is far too small to move them.
- **পৃথিবী** (the start): a globe (north up, longitude 0° facing the viewer, Bangladesh marked in the flag's green with
  a red dot at Dhaka) with four people on its edge, each holding an apple: you in Bangladesh (a sky-blue school shirt),
  people at both poles, and one exactly opposite Bangladesh, in a boat on the Pacific. Each has a violet arrow from
  the middle of the body towards the centre and a dashed line on to the centre: they all meet there. The student drags
  themself anywhere round the circle (on land, or in a boat at sea; the panel names the place), and taps
  "আপেল ছাড়ো": every apple falls at its person's feet, towards the centre, in about half a second (as from a hand
  about a metre up). The people stand on the circle through both poles along 90° E / 90° W, the edge of the globe in
  the front view, so they are always seen side-on.
- **চাঁদ** and **সূর্য**: the Moon round the Earth, the Earth round the Sun (lit by the Sun). "টান না থাকলে?" takes
  the pull away for about 4 s: the body leaves its circle along the tangent, in a straight line at the same speed
  (Newton's first law, page 64), with a dashed trail; then the pull comes back and it goes round again.
- **গ্যালাক্সি**: a spiral galaxy drawn as dots (a printed-diagram look on the white page), our Sun marked about
  half-way out with its pull towards the centre. "পাল্লা অসীম".

A strip at the foot of the learn panel shows the four fundamental forces weakest to strongest with their ranges
(অসীম, 10⁻¹⁸ m, অসীম, 10⁻¹⁵ m), মহাকর্ষ highlighted.

Honest notes in the corner: people drawn much bigger; force arrows drawn many times bigger; sizes, distances and time
not to scale; the galaxy's Sun drawn bigger and time much faster. The galaxy turns as a whole (stylised: real
galaxies turn faster near the middle).

The test (5 questions, 4 taps each, each scene set up for its question, the pulls hidden when they would give the
answer away, a worked answer that names the student's mistake): the direction of the pull (or of a dropped apple) for
you standing somewhere on the Earth, answered with four arrows (towards the centre, away, along the ground, down the
screen); what keeps the Moon or the Earth going round, or where the Moon would go if the pull vanished; which pull is
called মাধ্যাকর্ষণ বল, or why we feel our weight; do two apples attract each other; the range, or the pull at twice the
distance (the apple then moves out to 40 cm and the arrows shrink to a quarter).

## Numbers used
- G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²; apples 0.2 kg, 7 cm across, centres 20–40 cm apart: 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N … 1.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N.
- The Earth's radius about 6,400 km (6,371 km); the Moon about 3.84 lakh km away, one turn in about 27 days (27.3);
  the Sun about 15 crore km away (14.96), one turn a year; the Sun about 26 thousand light-years from the galaxy's
  centre, one turn in about 23 crore years (about 230 million).
- In the figure: the Moon turns once in 10 s, the Earth round the Sun in 14 s, the galaxy in 48 s.

## Open questions
- The book's strengths conflict (3.2.2: electromagnetic is 10³⁶ × gravity; 3.2.4: strong is 10⁴¹ × gravity and 100 ×
  electromagnetic, which would make it 10³⁸ × gravity). The strip therefore gives only the order, weakest to
  strongest, and no numbers. For the book team.
- মাধ্যাকর্ষণ বল: the book defines it as the Earth's gravitational force acting on us. The test only calls the
  Earth's pull on an apple মাধ্যাকর্ষণ and avoids the Earth's pull on the Moon, which some books also call মাধ্যাকর্ষণ
  (or অভিকর্ষ).
- The fourth chip is called "পৃথিবী" (people on the Earth); "আমরা" was the other choice.
- The "চার মৌলিক বল" title is the brief's (the page itself says "চার ধরনের বল" on p69).
