# Notes: Charge (আধান বা চার্জ)

Book: physics-9-10 › 10.1 আধান বা চার্জ (Charge) › pages 272–273. Reference: `reference/page-272.png`,
`reference/page-273.png`. The exact Bangla of the topic: `ch9-10-briefs/pages/10.1.md`.

## What the page shows
- p.272: the comb that lifts paper, a child's hair standing up on a carpet, lightning: all three come from চার্জ বা
  আধান. Everything is made of atoms; 118 elements; water, salt (একটা সোডিয়াম + একটা ক্লোরিন), methane. The atom: a small
  নিউক্লিয়াস of প্রোটন (ধনাত্মক বা পজিটিভ) and নিউট্রন (no charge), with ইলেকট্রন (ঋণাত্মক বা নেগেটিভ) round it; the
  proton's and electron's charges are equal and opposite (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ Coulomb); as many electrons as protons, so the
  atom is বিদ্যুৎ নিরপেক্ষ বা নিস্তড়িৎ বা নিউট্রাল. Hydrogen, helium.
- p.273: electrons are on different orbits (কক্ষপথ); চিত্র ১০.০১, "একটি আরগনের পরমাণুর মডেল। এক কক্ষপথ পূর্ণ করে
  ইলেকট্রন পরের কক্ষপথে যায়" (a flat Bohr picture: a red nucleus, three circles with 2, 8, 8 light-blue electrons).
  Inner electrons are held tightly; outer ones can be separated with a little effort, for example by ঘর্ষণ. Take one
  outer electron away and the atom has one positive charge, two give two: আয়নিত বা আহিত. An atom with one or two
  electrons more is negative. Conductors (সোনা, রুপা, তামা) and insulators (কাঠ, প্লাস্টিক, কাচ, রাবার).

## The one idea to teach
An atom has a positive nucleus (protons) and negative electrons round it; equal numbers make it neutral; an atom that
loses electrons is positively charged, one that gains electrons negatively charged.

## How the figure works
- **Scene** (cm of a display model; GLB in metres): the argon atom of চিত্র ১০.০১ floats over a plain round sand stand in
  the calm deep-blue setting (the stand only grounds it and catches its soft shadow). The nucleus is 18 protons (red
  #e03131, a white "+") and 22 neutrons (grey #9aa3ad, no sign; argon-40) packed into a ball; three thin light orbits in
  one plane leaning back 12° (the book's circles, readable as circles from the three-quarter view, about 28° to the
  side); 18 electrons (blue #1971c2, a white "−"), 2, 8, 8 at the book's places. Every ball turns to the viewer, so
  every proton shows its "+" and every electron its "−" from any angle (the look agreed with `coulombs-law`: red +,
  blue −, white signs). A small note on the view: "মডেল: মাপ ও কক্ষপথ প্রকৃত নয়" (the nucleus is drawn about 1/5 of the first orbit, electrons bigger than protons, and electrons do not really circle like planets).
- **Steps** (one tap each, slow, never auto-advancing; each step word jumps there):
  ① নিউক্লিয়াস দেখো: the camera flies into the nucleus (2 s), the electrons fade and the orbits dim; labels প্রোটন (+) and
     নিউট্রন; result "১৮টি প্রোটন (+) আর ২২টি নিউট্রন"; rule: প্রোটনের চার্জ **ধনাত্মক বা পজিটিভ**, নিউট্রনের চার্জ নেই.
  ② ইলেকট্রন বসাও: the camera comes back out while the 18 electrons fly in one by one (4.8 s), inner orbit first, each
     orbit full before the next (the book's caption), the orbits turning gently while they settle (then still); the
     panel's count fills live; the orbit counts ২টি, ৮টি, ৮টি; the atom's chip "আরগন: মোট চার্জ ০" and "নিউক্লিয়াস +১৮";
     rule: equal and opposite charges (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C), equal numbers: **বিদ্যুৎ নিরপেক্ষ বা নিস্তড়িৎ**. The numbered ring
     sits on the outer electron nearest the upper right, and the lead says to tap it: a tap there, or the button, takes it.
  ③ একটি ইলেকট্রন সরাও: one outer electron (upper right) flies out (2 s) and waits outside, a dashed ring marking its
     empty place; "সরানো ইলেকট্রন"; the chip turns red, "+১"; result "(+১৮) + (−১৭) = +১: একটি পজিটিভ চার্জ, +১.৬ ×
     ১০⁻¹⁹ C"; rule: one taken, one positive charge; two, two: **আয়নিত বা আহিত**. The lead counts ("আরও একটি বাইরের ইলেকট্রনে টোকা দাও (সরানো ১/৩), বা লবণের ক্লোরিনে চলো"). The student may now tap other outer
     electrons (up to 3; ↑/↓ keys too) or tap a taken one to bring it back; an inner electron shakes and the hint says
     the book's "ভেতরের কক্ষপথের ইলেকট্রন শক্তভাবে আটকে থাকে"; a tap on the nucleus says only electrons move.
  ④ ক্লোরিনে চলো: the argon atom shrinks away and a chlorine atom grows in its place (17 protons, 18 neutrons; 2-8-7,
     one dashed free place); a বিচ্ছিন্ন ইলেকট্রন waits outside, then drops into the free place (2 s): "যুক্ত হওয়া
     ইলেকট্রন", the chip turns blue, "−১"; result "(+১৭) + (−১৮) = −১: একটি নেগেটিভ চার্জ, −১.৬ × ১০⁻¹⁹ C"; rule: the
     book's "বিচ্ছিন্ন … ইলেকট্রন … যুক্ত হয়ে যায়, তখন পরমাণুর মোট চার্জ হয় নেগেটিভ". A tap on that electron sends it
     out again (neutral chlorine) and back; the lead says so.
  ⑤ পরিবাহী দেখো (page 273's last paragraph): the atom makes way for two cut-away rows of matter, as `src/matter.js`: তামা:
     সুপরিবাহী, whose free electrons (মুক্ত ইলেকট্রন) run through the whole piece with a short faint trail, and কাচ:
     অপরিবাহী, whose electrons stay on their atoms. The atoms are plain grey in both rows, so which row conducts is
     read from the electrons alone. They run 7 s (half each way: random motion, not a current); a tap runs them again.
     Rule: সোনা, রুপা, তামা; কাঠ, প্লাস্টিক, কাচ, রাবার.
- **The count of charges** in the panel (the protons' row alone at ①, where the close-up can't show all 18): a row of 18 red "+" discs over a row of blue "−" discs, paired column by column;
  a charge without a partner is outlined (it is the net charge), a taken electron is a dashed empty disc. It follows
  the 3D live (as electrons arrive, leave or join). The result line starts with the coloured sum.
- **Panel**: a calm first card (comb, lightning, "সামনে একটি আরগন পরমাণুর মডেল…", a short version for small frames), then
  per step a lead-in line, the action, the count, the result and the rule. The kit's how-to row (turn, front, "পরমাণুতে
  টোকা দাও" / "ইলেকট্রনে টোকা দাও"), dropped where the panel is short (phones upright, 844 × 390, 640 × 400), as in
  `coulombs-law`. Taps on the 3D are real taps (≤ 8 px, so a drag still turns the view).
- **Test** (6 questions, 4 tap choices, each wrong choice with its own note, reshuffled each time; every question sets up
  its own scene and needs it, labels hidden; no numbers in the question text): ① the nucleus close up: what are the
  particles with no sign (parallel choices)? ② the neutral atom: how many electrons on the innermost orbit? ③ argon
  with an electron outside and its place dashed: which particle left? ④ argon with two outer electrons outside: its
  total charge (+২ / −২ / ০ / +১৮)? ⑤ neutral chlorine with the stray electron waiting: the charge in coulombs when it
  joins (it joins on the reveal)? ⑥ the two rows, the metal at the top or bottom at random: which conducts?

## Decisions
- **Chlorine for the negative case.** Argon (the book's figure) has a full outer orbit and does not form a stable negative
  ion; its positive ion is real (Ar⁺, as in a discharge lamp). The book's words for the negative case are general
  ("একটা পরমাণু … নেগেটিভভাবেও আয়নিত হতে পারে"), so step ④ uses chlorine, which the same page names (লবণ = সোডিয়াম +
  ক্লোরিন) and which really takes one electron (Cl⁻, 2-8-8). Question for the orchestrator/book team: keep, or show the
  negative case on argon with a note?
- Isotopes: argon-40 (22 neutrons) and chlorine-35 (18 neutrons), the common ones; the book gives no neutron counts.
- The book's "৪০টি মৌলের পরমাণু … স্থায়ী" is not repeated (about 80 elements have a stable isotope); see INDEX.md slips.
- e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C as the book; one charge in the panel is shown both as "+১" (the book's "একটি পজিটিভ চার্জ") and in
  coulombs.
- Not shown in 3D: friction (10.2's figure, `charging-by-friction`); question ③'s worked answer names ঘর্ষণ.
- ⑤ shows copper and glass with their atoms as plain equal balls: the picture teaches free against bound electrons, not
  the materials' structure. Real copper has about one free electron per atom, which is what the row shows.
- At 390 × 520 the panel keeps the whole first card (not `intro_short`): it fits, and a short one left white below it.
- What to tap is signposted on phones, where the how-to row is dropped: the ring in ②, the lead in ③, ④ and ⑤. A step word chosen while another step plays jumps from where things are.
- At most three outer electrons can be taken in the figure (the book speaks of one or two).

## Look
Calm deep-blue gradient backdrop (`['#0b2a5b', '#123d7a']`), a plain sand stand; panel white. Colours: proton and a
positive total red #e03131, electron and a negative total blue #1971c2, neutron and a zero total grey; orbits light
blue-white. Short labels only on the 3D (a white chip in the colour of what it names); sentences in the panel.

## Performance (0.1.0; headless Chromium, software GL, 844 × 390, shadows on)
Draw calls / triangles in one frame (the shadow pass included): intro and electrons 12 / 67 k, nucleus 10 / 55 k, three
electrons taken 14 / 69 k, chlorine 12 / 62 k, conductors 13 / 53 k. 0 draws while idle (the orbits turn only while
electrons settle, the free electrons run only for 7 s). No shader is linked after load: every program is compiled at
start, and `stage.warmUp` shows the hidden empty places and the rows of matter. Instanced: one draw each for protons,
neutrons, electrons, empty places, and in ⑤ for the atoms, the electrons and the trails; the three orbits are one mesh.
Spheres: nucleons 20 × 14 segments, electrons 16 × 12 (their GLB size too; most nucleons are hidden inside the nucleus).
JS per animated frame at 6× CPU throttling, measured on the four-step version: median 0.1 ms, p90 1 ms, p99 2 ms.
GLB: the atom model (as verify reports it).
