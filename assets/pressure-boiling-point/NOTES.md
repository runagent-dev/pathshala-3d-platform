# Notes: Pressure and boiling point: mountain and pressure cooker (চাপের ফলে স্ফুটনাঙ্ক)

Book: physics-9-10 › 6.7 › p182 (reference pictures: `reference/page-181.png`, `reference/page-182.png`)

## What the page shows
Topic 6.7 "গলনাঙ্ক এবং স্ফুটনাঙ্কের ওপর চাপের প্রভাব". After the melting-point paragraphs and চিত্র ৬.০৯ (the wire through

- "চাপের কারণে স্ফুটনাঙ্কের পরিবর্তন হয়। চাপ কম হলে স্ফুটনাঙ্ক কমে যায়, চাপ বেশি হলে স্ফুটনাঙ্ক বেড়ে যায়।"
- Climbers high up: air pressure is low, so water boils at a lower temperature, the temperature cannot be raised, so
  cooking takes longer.
- The pressure cooker, "একটি নিশ্ছিদ্র পাত্র": trapped steam raises the pressure, the boiling point rises, water boils at a
  higher temperature, cooking is quicker.
- A gas under pressure can be liquefied without cooling it very much, but then much heat is made and must be removed.
Then the sample questions (an unrelated rail-gap question: not for this figure).

**This figure brings those paragraphs to life with the book's own two examples**, in one setting (a pot of water on a
stove) with an open pot as the usual case to compare with.

## The one idea to teach
Pressure changes the boiling point: low pressure lowers it, high pressure raises it.

## What the student does
Pick the place (open pot, high on a mountain, pressure cooker), then one big button: light the fire. The water heats
and the thermometer's red thread stops at that place's boiling point (the usual 100° C marked). Red arrows on the water
show the pressure (short on the mountain, longer in the cooker). After boiling the same button shows the next place.
The book's gas sentence appears as a small note after the cooker has been seen.

তাপমাত্রা, গ্যাস, তরল. The page writes the unit as "° C" (e.g. 30° C).

## Choices made
- The book gives **no temperatures** for this topic. 100° C is the usual boiling point of water; 90 and 120 are round
  figures for the mountain and the cooker, always shown as "প্রায়" (about). They are not from the page.
- The pot has a glass body (steel base and rim) so the water, bubbles and thermometer show; a real cooker is steel
  (cut-away). The thermometer passes through the lid with a seal.
- No mountain scenery (nothing decorative): the case is told by the chip, the arrows and the labels.
- Bubbles move while the water heats and for six seconds of boiling, then the picture rests (nothing draws when idle).
- The unit is "° C" glued to the number as the book prints it ("৩০° C").
