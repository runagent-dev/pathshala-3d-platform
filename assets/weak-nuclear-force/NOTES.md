# Notes: Weak nuclear force (দুর্বল নিউক্লীয় বল)

Book: physics-9-10 › 3.2.3 › p68 (screenshot: `reference/page-68.png`, the third section of the page)

3.2.2 তড়িৎ চৌম্বক বল, 3.2.3 দুর্বল নিউক্লীয় বল. The 3.2.3 paragraph, exactly:

## The one idea to teach
The weak nuclear force is weaker than the electromagnetic force (about a trillion times) but far stronger than
gravity; it acts only over a tiny distance (10⁻¹⁸ m), and it is why a radioactive nucleus shoots out a beta (β) ray,
an electron.

## Terms (as in the book)
দুর্বল নিউক্লীয় বল, তড়িৎ চৌম্বক বল, মহাকর্ষ বল, দূরত্ব, 10⁻¹⁸ m, তেজস্ক্রিয় নিউক্লিয়াস, বিটা (β) রশ্মি, ইলেকট্রন,
ট্রিলিয়ন গুণ. From the neighbouring sections: পাল্লা (range: "মহাকর্ষ বলের পাল্লা অসীম"), সবল নিউক্লীয় বল (3.2.4).
Also used, from the students' chemistry: প্রোটন, নিউট্রন, পরমাণু.

## How the figure works
- **The hero, β emission**: a radioactive nucleus, 6 protons (red #d23a50, "+") and 8 neutrons (grey #9aa3ad), as in
  carbon-14 (a real β emitter; not named in the figure); five of the protons face the front, so a student counting
  sees most of them. Tap it (the first-use hand points at it) or press "β রশ্মি দেখো": a green spark where the weak
  force acts inside one neutron (labelled "দুর্বল নিউক্লীয় বল" while it shows), then the neutron turns red with a
  "+" just as the blue electron with its "−" is made (charge kept), and the electron flies out in a straight line,
  leaving a track, and stops inside the view (about 85% of the way to the edge, measured on the screen at every
  size). When the spark ends, the force's label gives way to "নিউট্রন → প্রোটন" on the changed ball. The panel says it
  in the book's words and counts protons 6 → 7, neutrons 8 → 7. "সময় অনেক ধীর করে দেখানো" in the corner.
- **How short the reach is**: three zoom stops (chips): পরমাণু (the cloud, with a line "পরমাণু: প্রায় 10⁻¹⁰ m"; the
  panel: the electromagnetic force holds the electrons from any distance, the weak force's reach is a hundred
  millionth of this), নিউক্লিয়াস, দুর্বল বলের পাল্লা (the zoom dives a thousand times into the neutron, slowly: about a
  second per power of ten inside it, to a green sphere of radius 10⁻¹⁸ m; the panel: "if a neutron were a football,
  this range would be a grain of sand"). A scale bar (always a power of ten) follows the zoom; inside the neutron it
  also says "নিউট্রনের ১০ / ১০০ / ১০০০ ভাগের এক ভাগ". With the pointers on, the figure opens on the atom and zooms
  into the nucleus by itself.
- **Strength**: a strip "চার মৌলিক বল (সবচেয়ে দুর্বল → সবচেয়ে শক্তিশালী)" (the heading the four force figures share),
  weakest first (forces.json order and colours), the weak force filled in, each with its range (পাল্লা অসীম / 10⁻¹⁸ m
  / অসীম / 10⁻¹⁵ m), and the book's sentence under it. No numeric strength scale (see the open question).
- **Test (5 questions)**: always first, which force causes the β ray (the emission plays with a neutral spark and no
  force label; later scenes show the force in green, so this one cannot be answered from them); then, shuffled: what
  the β ray is (electron / proton / neutron / light; the answer replays the emission) or how many protons are left
  after it (the labels give "প্রোটন: ৬টি", "নিউট্রন: ৮টি"; once the zoom is there the answer plays and the numbers
  change); the range (10⁻¹⁸ / 10⁻¹⁵ / 10⁻¹⁰ m / any distance, the sphere's label hidden; 10⁻¹⁵ m is named as the
  strong force's range) or which of gravity and the weak force acts from far away; how many times smaller the reach
  is than a neutron (the question gives 10⁻¹⁵ m, the picture inside the neutron shows 10⁻¹⁸ m; 10 / 100 / 1000 / 10
  lakh times; asked after the range question when both come); strength ("called weak, but stronger than which?" or
  where it goes when the four are ordered). Worked answers lead with the answer and name the mistake.
- Sizes are honest where they can be: the nucleus (radius about 2.9 fm) and the reach (10⁻¹⁸ m) are to scale with
  the scale bar; at the atom stop the nucleus and electrons are drawn much bigger (the note says so).

## Decisions
- The scene is drawn in femtometres. The camera never moves through the powers of ten: each frame the world is
  scaled by 1/R and shifted so the point looked at is at the origin (as in si-prefixes).
- The book says only that β rays are electrons and that the weak force causes them. The figure adds one step
  students can count: a neutron becomes a proton when the electron leaves. It does not show the antineutrino that
  also leaves (not in the book; see the open questions), and the panel never writes it as an equation.
- The weak force's reach is drawn round a point inside the changing neutron (where the change happens). Quarks
  are not shown (not in the book). The first version had a "where does it act?" question whose answer ("between the
  particles inside the nucleus") was really the strong force's place; it was replaced by the "how many times
  smaller than a neutron" question.
- GLB: the nucleus only (6 protons, 8 neutrons), with the scene's femtometres written as centimetres (asset.json
  units "cm", as in strong-nuclear-force): the file is 10¹³ times life size (a nucleus about 6 cm across), since a
  model at its real size (10⁻¹⁵ m) shows nothing in any viewer. The β parts, the atom and the reach sphere are not
  exported.

## Open questions
- The book's strength numbers do not agree (3.2.2: EM is 10³⁶ × gravity; 3.2.4: strong is 10⁴¹ × gravity and
  100 × EM, which would make it 10³⁸ × gravity). The four figures show strength only as an order (forces.json) and
  quote each section's own sentence. For the book team.
- β decay also gives out an antineutrino; the book does not mention it, so the figure does not either. Should a
  later edition say "an electron (and a very light particle)"?
- The book writes 10⁻¹⁸ m with Latin digits; the figure follows the repo rule (Bangla digits, ১০⁻¹⁸ m). A person
  should confirm that this reads naturally.
