# Notes: Newton's third law of motion (নিউটনের তৃতীয় গতি সূত্র)

Book: physics-9-10 › 3.8 › p84–86 (the walking example at the end of 3.8, page 86, is a separate figure, `walking-third-law`)

## What the page shows
Section ৩.৮ **নিউটনের তৃতীয় গতি সূত্র (Newton's Third Law of Motion)**. It starts: the first law tells what happens when no force
acts, the second what a force does; "যখন একটি বস্তু অন্য বস্তুর ওপর বল প্রয়োগ করে তখন বস্তু দুটির মাঝে কী ধরনের প্রতিক্রিয়া হয়, সেটি
আমরা জানতে পারব নিউটনের তৃতীয় সূত্র থেকে।" The boxed law (word for word, the last step of the rule line):
"নিউটনের তৃতীয় সূত্র: যখন একটি বস্তু অন্য একটি বস্তুর ওপর বল প্রয়োগ করে তখন সেই বস্তুটিও প্রথম বস্তুটির ওপর বিপরীত দিকে সমান
বল প্রয়োগ করে।"
Then: physics books write "প্রত্যেক ক্রিয়ার (Action) একটা সমান এবং বিপরীত প্রতিক্রিয়া (Reaction) থাকে"; the book does not, because
calling a force "ক্রিয়া" or "প্রতিক্রিয়া" is confusing, and the first question a new student asks is: if every action has an
opposite reaction, why do the two not cancel? The page answers: "তৃতীয় সূত্র বলছে যে যদি দুটি বস্তু A এবং B থাকে তাহলে A যখন B বলের ওপর
বল প্রয়োগ করে তখন B বল প্রয়োগ করে A এর ওপর। বিপরীত দুটি বল ভিন্ন ভিন্ন বস্তুতে কাজ করে, কখনোই এক বস্তুতে নয়। যদি একই বস্তুতে দুটি বল
প্রয়োগ করা হতো, শুধু তাহলেই একে অন্যকে কাটাকাটি করতে পারত। এখানে কাটাকাটির কোনো সুযোগ নেই।"
The example (চিত্র ৩.১২, an apple above the Earth with an arrow F down at the apple and an arrow F up at the Earth): an apple
of mass m is let go; the Earth pulls it with F = G mM/R² = mg; by the third law the apple pulls the whole Earth with the same
F in the opposite direction; "আমরা এই বলটিকে নিয়ে মাথা ঘামাই না, তার কারণ এই বলটার কারণে পৃথিবীর কতটুকু ত্বরণ a হচ্ছে সেটা ইচ্ছে করলে
বের করতে পারি: F = Ma", so a = F/M = mg/M = (m/M)g; "যদি পৃথিবীর ভর M = 5.98 × 10²⁴ kg হয় তাহলে আমরা যদি 1 kg ভরের একটা বস্তুর উপর
থেকে ছেড়ে দিই তার জন্য পৃথিবীর ত্বরণ হবে—" then the page turns and the next page starts "এটি এত ক্ষুদ্র যে কেউ এটা নিয়ে মাথা ঘামায় না" and
adds that when you jump down you pulled the whole Earth toward you. Screenshots: `reference/page-84.png` (the topic's start and
the apple), `reference/page-86.png` (the end of the apple example and the walking example).

## The one idea to teach
Forces come in equal and opposite pairs, and each force of a pair acts on a DIFFERENT body, so they never cancel; the apple and
the Earth pull each other with the same force, but the Earth is so heavy that it hardly moves.

## What the figure does
- Pairs as one row of words: **সমান ভর** (two skaters of 50 kg), **হালকা ও ভারী** (50 kg and 100 kg), **আপেল ও পৃথিবী** (the book's
  চিত্র ৩.১২). One big numbered action ("১ ধাক্কা দাও" / "আপেল ছেড়ে দাও"), its twin badge ringing the skaters' hands (or the
  apple): a tap on either, on a skater, on the apple, Space/Enter/→ is the same action. After a pair is done the button offers the
  next pair not tried yet; the number is the student's own step.
- Skaters: they stand face to face, hands together, on a smooth floor and push each other with the SAME steady 10 N until the
  hands have moved 0.4 m apart (real time, about 1.4–1.6 s). While the hands touch there is an arrow on each body, always equally
  long, pointing away from the other skater, named on the body it acts on ("A-এর ওপর বল", "B-এর ওপর বল"). The two forces of a pair
  are the same kind of force, so both are orange (the push; the apple's pair is gravity violet) and are told apart by position,
  name and a solid arrow (A's push, on B) against a dashed one (B's push back, on A), with the same solid or dashed swatch in the
  label: never colour alone. Afterwards there is no force ("ধাক্কা শেষ: এখন কোনো বল নেই, তাই দুজনের বেগ আর বদলাচ্ছে না") and each goes on
  at the speed it reached; the faster one leaves the picture, then they stand ready again. A small mark on the floor shows where each
  started and a bar from it how far each has gone, so "twice as far" is seen on the floor, not only read. a = F/m: with equal masses both move equally (0.2 ms⁻²);
  with 50 kg and 100 kg the lighter goes twice as far (0.2 and 0.1 ms⁻²). Result line: "দুজন সমান সরে গেল" / "হালকা A সরে গেল ভারী
  B-র চেয়ে ২ গুণ বেশি" (a comparison first, no formula).
- Apple: held in a hand above a ball-shaped Earth; the fingers open, the hand lifts away, the apple falls (1.4 m in the picture,
  time 2× slower, the note says so) and lands. While it falls: an arrow on the apple (down) and an arrow on the Earth (up), both
  9.8 N, equally long (gravity violet), each named on its body. Result: "আপেল নিচে নামল, কিন্তু পৃথিবী নড়ল না বললেই চলে"; after the
  landing the Earth's tag ("পৃথিবীর ত্বরণ …") shows its acceleration (1.64 × 10⁻²⁴ ms⁻²) for the one who wants the number: no powers of ten in
  the first views. The feel for its size is one plain sentence in the rule ("পৃথিবীর ভর আপেলের চেয়ে প্রায় ৬-এর পরে ২৪টি শূন্য বসালে যত, তত গুণ
  বেশি": 5.98 × 10²⁴ ≈ 6 followed by 24 zeros); M and the formulas appear only in the test's worked answer.
- The rule line grows in plain words (a question first, then "A ধাক্কা দেয় B-কে, B-ও ধাক্কা দেয় A-কে, সমান জোরে", then "বল সমান, তবু হালকা জন
  বেশি সরে", then the apple) and ends on the book's boxed law word for word once all three pairs have been seen; at that step the
  force labels take the book's names: F and −F for the two skaters (right is positive, as `walking-third-law`), F on both for the apple, as the page's picture labels it.
- The action keeps its own words while the first run of a pair goes on ("১ ধাক্কা দাও"; "আবার …" only for a pair already seen). When it offers
  a pair that is not on the picture, no ring sits on the picture (the dotted word in the row says what comes next).
- Test (5, each on its own scene), in an order that never gives a later answer away: the size question first (its answer, "equal", is in
  the later worked answers), the apple's reaction before the Earth's question. Size: the picture shows only the force on A with its number
  (10 N), the question is how big the force on B is (0 / 5 / 10 / 20 N, in order); reaction: what the apple does to the Earth (the apple
  falling, only the Earth's pull drawn); cancel: why two equal opposite forces do not cancel (skaters mid-push, both arrows); who moves
  more (masses on the picture); why the Earth does not visibly move. The worked answers of the reaction, cancel and size questions keep the
  scene still with both arrows on while they are read. Wrong choices are the real mistakes (the small one does not pull the big one, the
  forces cancel, the floor holds one back, one force first, mass divides the force, the force doubles, no force back, the ground's push).

## Terms (as in the book)
নিউটনের তৃতীয় সূত্র, বল প্রয়োগ, ক্রিয়া (Action), প্রতিক্রিয়া (Reaction), সমান, বিপরীত দিকে, ভিন্ন ভিন্ন বস্তু, কাটাকাটি, আকর্ষণ,
পৃথিবীর ভর M, ত্বরণ, ওপর (this page spells it ওপর). Our own words: স্কেটার is not used on screen (A and B, the book's letters, with their
masses), হালকা/ভারী, ধাক্কা দাও (the page's verb), আপেল ছেড়ে দাও, "মসৃণ মেঝে: ঘর্ষণ ধরা হয়নি".

## Decisions
- Plain words first (the owner's "explain more easily", 2026-10-26): the panel says who pushes whom and "সমান জোরে" before any symbol; F is
  named only at the last step; a = F/m and M appear only in the test's worked answers.
- The picture is not to scale and says so: the apple is drawn much bigger than a real one and the Earth a few metres across, so its
  curve shows; the apple's time is 2× slower. The skaters' picture is in real time (smooth floor, friction ignored).
- F and −F for the two skaters but F and F for the apple, on purpose: page 86 prints F and −F for the walking pair (`walking-third-law`),
  page 84's printed picture of the apple labels both arrows F. The names come only at the last step, with one bridging sentence.
- The pair colours: both forces of a pair are the SAME hue (a push or pull orange; gravity violet), told apart by position, name and a solid
  vs dashed arrow. `walking-third-law` draws its pair in orange and crimson because the ground's push on a walker IS static friction (crimson
  is friction in this chapter); here neither force is friction.
- The 1 kg apple is the book's "1 kg ভরের একটা বস্তু" (a real apple is about 0.1–0.2 kg; a = (m/M)g scales with m).
- Colours: a push or pull orange, gravity violet, for BOTH forces of a pair (crimson is friction and teal acceleration in this chapter); acceleration teal only in the result line's number.

## Replacement requested by the owner (v1.0.0)
The equal/unequal skater tabs and models are replaced by **নৌকা থেকে লাফ**: a complete articulated schoolboy jumps
from a hollow planked wooden boat onto a jetty; the boat recoils. During the 0.35 s push, horizontal forces are
+300 N on the 50 kg boy and −300 N on the 150 kg boat. The boy accelerates forward and the boat backward; horizontal
momentum is conserved until the boy lands on the external jetty. Only these horizontal contact forces are drawn
(the note states this; water resistance is ignored). After takeoff those force arrows disappear; separately named
cyan velocity arrows show motion. Vertical flight is ballistic after takeoff; the initial upward speed represents
the leg push, whose vertical force components and buoyancy are outside the horizontal diagram.

The apple view now shows the WHOLE globe with NASA's locally bundled Blue Marble map, an enlarged red apple,
equal-length solid/dashed gravity arrows in one diagram plane, and a centre-to-centre R guide. The learner sees
F = GmM/R² = mg, F_m = −F_M as vectors, a_m = F/m = g and a_M = F/M = (m/M)g ≈ 1.64×10⁻²⁴ m/s²,
with m = 1 kg and M = 5.98×10²⁴ kg. The picture is explicitly not to scale. It pauses BEFORE surface contact, avoiding
an unshown normal force in a lesson about the free-fall pair; both gravitational forces remain visible in the paused
moment. Both examples run at half speed. Credit and provenance are in media/README.md; no network is needed at runtime.

Version 1.0.0 is intentional: example IDs equal/unequal are removed and replaced by boat; apple remains apple.
The old skater-specific snapshot/tried API and questions are removed. `at` is now physical simulation seconds;
`push` starts either the jump or release. The five questions use only the new boat/apple scenes.

The owner's follow-up asks for simpler-looking maths. The default apple sheet now shows only equal force,
apple acceleration and Earth acceleration, with short names and results. A keyboard-accessible **পূর্ণ হিসাব**
button opens a separate calculation sheet with the full inverse-square equation, vector pair, masses and constants.
This leaves the picture and default explanation uncluttered; the derivation remains complete and offline.

## Planted push and bow dip (v1.0.1)
Both sneaker soles stay fixed on a dedicated wooden footboard in boat coordinates throughout contact.
The hips travel forward as the knees bend and the arms swing; the feet leave only when the contact pair ends.
The bow dips by about five degrees under the push, then gently rebounds and settles after takeoff; the wake
stays on the water. This pitch/heave is an illustrative load response, not a quantified buoyancy model.
The release height follows the dipped deck, with a continuous transition to ballistic flight and a softer
landing pose. The horizontal force, momentum and simple formula presentation are unchanged.

## Clearer vector arrows (v1.0.2)
Force and motion arrows have thicker shafts, larger heads and a dark outline; their unlit colours stay vivid
over clothing, wood and water. The two horizontal force arrows remain equal in length (now 0.85 scene units),
with matching gold colour and solid/dashed shafts. The boy's force begins at his forward side so more of it is

## Guided apple–Earth lesson (v1.1.0)
The owner found the one-click fall and formulas insufficient to teach the idea. Apple learning now proceeds
through three paused stages: a fall at 6× slower time with a ghost start and distance trail; a comparison naming
Earth → apple and apple → Earth with the same 9.8 N on different bodies; and a two-choice explanation of why
Earth barely moves. A wrong smaller-force answer points back to the equal values and allows retry. Only after
the correct mass explanation does the main sheet show the two acceleration results. The complete derivation
remains behind Full calculation. Both arrows remain simultaneous, with a persistent panel legend when labels
need to hide on a phone. Earth is not moved by an exaggerated amount. R is reserved for the calculation view.
The bridge retains push/release and physical at; optional lessonStep (0–2) selects a paused teaching stage.
Boat behavior is unchanged; home and example changes restart the apple lesson, and quiz mode owns its scene.

The final stage uses a qualitative acceleration comparison and the single relation a = F/m. Scientific notation
and the complete numerical acceleration results stay in the calculation sheet. Compact phones omit a repeated
rule paragraph (already taught in the legend/feedback) to leave more room for an enlarged apple. The ghost
becomes visible only once it separates from the real apple, preventing two superimposed apples during early fall.

The comparison step reveals both force values together; the observation step names who pulls whom without
numbers. The paused-fall explanation belongs in the panel rather than a third line over the small-screen object.

## Gravity label layout (v1.1.1)
The two gravity arrows use separate lanes beside the apple and upper Earth. Their cards name the agent and
recipient on short lines and connect horizontally to their own shafts. Each card stays outside the pair;
when the camera orbits behind the scene, the card sides swap with the projected arrow order. Geometry bounds
keep all apple-scene labels clear of the real apple, Earth, start-position ghost and both arrow shapes.
