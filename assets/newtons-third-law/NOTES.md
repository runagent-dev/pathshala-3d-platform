# Notes: Newton's third law of motion (নিউটনের তৃতীয় গতি সূত্র)

Book: physics-9-10 › 3.8 › p84–86 (the walking example at the end of 3.8, page 86, is a separate figure, `walking-third-law`)

## What the page shows
Section ৩.৮ **নিউটনের তৃতীয় গতি সূত্র (Newton's Third Law of Motion)**. It starts: the first law tells what happens when no force
acts, the second what a force does; "যখন একটি বস্তু অন্য বস্তুর ওপর বল প্রয়োগ করে তখন বস্তু দুটির মাঝে কী ধরনের প্রতিক্রিয়া হয়, সেটি
আমরা জানতে পারব নিউটনের তৃতীয় সূত্র থেকে।" The boxed law (word for word, the last step of the rule line):
"নিউটনের তৃতীয় সূত্র: যখন একটি বস্তু অন্য একটি বস্তুর ওপর বল প্রয়োগ করে তখন সেই বস্তুটিও প্রথম বস্তুটির ওপর বিপরীত দিকে সমান
বল প্রয়োগ করে।"
Then: physics books write "প্রত্যেক ক্রিয়ার (Action) একটা সমান এবং বিপরীত প্রতিক্রিয়া (Reaction) থাকে"; the book does not, because
calling a force "ক্রিয়া" or "প্রতিক্রিয়া" is confusing, and the first question a new student asks is: if every action has an
opposite reaction, why do the two not cancel? The page answers: "তৃতীয় সূত্র বলছে যে যদি দুটি বস্তু A এবং B থাকে তাহলে A যখন B বলের ওপর
বল প্রয়োগ করে তখন B বল প্রয়োগ করে A এর ওপর। বিপরীত দুটি বল ভিন্ন ভিন্ন বস্তুতে কাজ করে, কখনোই এক বস্তুতে নয়। যদি একই বস্তুতে দুটি বল
প্রয়োগ করা হতো, শুধু তাহলেই একে অন্যকে কাটাকাটি করতে পারত। এখানে কাটাকাটির কোনো সুযোগ নেই।"
The example (চিত্র ৩.১২, an apple above the Earth with an arrow F down at the apple and an arrow F up at the Earth): an apple
of mass m is let go; the Earth pulls it with F = G mM/R² = mg; by the third law the apple pulls the whole Earth with the same
F in the opposite direction; "আমরা এই বলটিকে নিয়ে মাথা ঘামাই না, তার কারণ এই বলটার কারণে পৃথিবীর কতটুকু ত্বরণ a হচ্ছে সেটা ইচ্ছে করলে
বের করতে পারি: F = Ma", so a = F/M = mg/M = (m/M)g; "যদি পৃথিবীর ভর M = 5.98 × 10²⁴ kg হয় তাহলে আমরা যদি 1 kg ভরের একটা বস্তুর উপর
থেকে ছেড়ে দিই তার জন্য পৃথিবীর ত্বরণ হবে—" then the page turns and the next page starts "এটি এত ক্ষুদ্র যে কেউ এটা নিয়ে মাথা ঘামায় না" and
adds that when you jump down you pulled the whole Earth toward you. Screenshots: `reference/page-84.png` (the topic's start and
the apple), `reference/page-86.png` (the end of the apple example and the walking example).

## The one idea to teach
Forces come in equal and opposite pairs, and each force of a pair acts on a DIFFERENT body, so they never cancel; the apple and
the Earth pull each other with the same force, but the Earth is so heavy that it hardly moves.

## What the figure does
- Pairs as one row of words: **সমান ভর** (two skaters of 50 kg), **হালকা ও ভারী** (50 kg and 100 kg), **আপেল ও পৃথিবী** (the book's
  চিত্র ৩.১২). One big numbered action ("১ ধাক্কা দাও" / "আপেল ছেড়ে দাও"), its twin badge ringing the skaters' hands (or the
  apple): a tap on either, on a skater, on the apple, Space/Enter/→ is the same action. After a pair is done the button offers the
  next pair not tried yet; the number is the student's own step.
- Skaters: they stand face to face, hands together, on a smooth floor and push each other with the SAME steady 10 N until the
  hands have moved 0.4 m apart (real time, about 1.4–1.6 s). While the hands touch there is an arrow on each body, always equally
  long, pointing away from the other skater, named on the body it acts on ("A-এর ওপর বল", "B-এর ওপর বল"). The two forces of a pair
  are the same kind of force, so both are orange (the push; the apple's pair is gravity violet) and are told apart by position,
  name and a solid arrow (A's push, on B) against a dashed one (B's push back, on A), with the same solid or dashed swatch in the
  label: never colour alone. Afterwards there is no force and each goes on at the speed it
  reached; the faster one leaves the picture, then they stand ready again. a = F/m: with equal masses both move equally (0.2 ms⁻²);
  with 50 kg and 100 kg the lighter goes twice as far (0.2 and 0.1 ms⁻²). Result line: "দুজন সমান সরে গেল" / "হালকা A সরে গেল ভারী
  B-র চেয়ে ২ গুণ বেশি" (a comparison first, no formula).
- Apple: held in a hand above a ball-shaped Earth; the fingers open, the hand lifts away, the apple falls (1.4 m in the picture,
  time 2× slower, the note says so) and lands. While it falls: an arrow on the apple (down) and an arrow on the Earth (up), both
  9.8 N, equally long (gravity violet), each named on its body. Result: "আপেল নিচে নামল, কিন্তু পৃথিবী নড়ল না বললেই চলে"; after the
  landing the Earth's tag shows its acceleration (1.64 × 10⁻²⁴ ms⁻²) for the one who wants the number: no powers of ten in the
  first views, M and the formulas only at the end (the test's worked answer).
- The rule line grows in plain words (a question first, then "A ঠেলে B-কে, B-ও ঠেলে A-কে, সমান জোরে", then "বল সমান, তবু হালকা জন
  বেশি সরে", then the apple) and ends on the book's boxed law word for word once all three pairs have been seen; at that step the
  force labels take the book's names: F and −F for the two skaters (right is positive, as `walking-third-law`), F on both for the apple, as the page's picture labels it.
- Test (5, each on its own scene, the size question always before the cancelling one): what the apple does to the Earth (the apple
  falling, only the Earth's pull drawn), why two equal opposite forces do not cancel (skaters mid-push, both arrows), who moves more
  (masses on the picture), on whom the force is bigger (heavy and light), why the Earth does not visibly move. Wrong choices are the
  real mistakes (the small one does not pull the big one, the forces cancel, friction, one force first, more mass means more force, the
  one who starts pushes harder, the ground's push).

## Decisions
- Plain words first (the owner's "explain more easily", 2026-10-26): the panel says who pushes whom and "সমান জোরে" before any symbol; F is
  named only at the last step; a = F/m and M appear only in the test's worked answers.
- The picture is not to scale and says so: the apple is drawn much bigger than a real one and the Earth a few metres across, so its
  curve shows; the apple's time is 2× slower. The skaters' picture is in real time (smooth floor, friction ignored).
- The 1 kg apple is the book's "1 kg ভরের একটা বস্তু" (a real apple is about 0.1–0.2 kg; a = (m/M)g scales with m).
- Colours: a push or pull orange, gravity violet, for BOTH forces of a pair (crimson is friction and teal acceleration in this chapter); acceleration teal only in the result line's number.
