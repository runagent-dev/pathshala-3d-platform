# Notes: Expansion of gas (গ্যাসের প্রসারণ)

Book: physics-9-10, topic 6.3.3, pages 173-174 (page images in `reference/page-173.png`, `page-174.png`).
The "নিজে করো" with two balloons on page 175 is its own figure, `balloons-hot-water`.

## What the page shows
- p173, চিত্র ৬.০৭ "তাপ প্রয়োগ করলে বাতাসের আয়তন বেড়ে যায়": two cylinders side by side, each with a piston (light
  with darker vertical bands) and a fixed weight on it (a grey trapezoid with a ring on top). The left piston is low, the
  right one is higher and a burner (a blue stick with an orange flame) is under the right cylinder. The text: a gas has
  no fixed shape and no fixed volume; the same amount of gas in different volumes has different pressure; to measure
  the increase in volume the pressure must not change, so a fixed weight on the piston gives the same pressure always.
- p174: the box `PV = nRT` (P pressure, V volume, n amount in moles, R = 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, T in kelvin), then the
  coefficient of volume expansion at constant pressure γ_P = ((V₂ − V₁)/V₁)/(T₂ − T₁), worked out to γ_P = 1/T₁, and
  the words: not a constant, the inverse of the temperature; the lower the temperature the more the gas expands; the
  same one degree of heating expands a gas more at a lower temperature.

## The one idea to teach
At the same pressure and amount, heating a gas makes its volume grow with the temperature (V = nRT/P), and the share
it grows by per kelvin is γ_P = 1/T₁: the colder the gas starts, the more it expands in proportion.

## What the figure does
- The book's picture, in 3D: two glass cylinders on lab tripods, striped pistons, grey weights with a ring, a burner
  under the right one. The same 36 molecules in both: they spread out when the piston rises.
- Chips choose the starting temperature T₁ (200, 300, 400 K). The big button (or a tap on the burner/cylinder) heats the
  right gas by 100 K over about 3 s; the second step cools it again. Under each chip, once tried: the expansion in %
  (+50, +33, +25).
- Panel order: tabs → T₁ chips → action → readings (PV = nRT box, T and V before/after, P and n unchanged) → the book's
  γ_P fraction with the student's numbers (V₂ − V₁)/V₁ over T₂ − T₁ = 1/T₁ → the rule (the book's words).
- The numbers: n = 0.12 mol and P = 100,000 Pa fixed, V = nRT/P in litres (about T/100 L). The same 100 K gives the
  same rise in litres (1 L) for every start, but a smaller V₁ makes the ratio bigger: the panel says so.
- Test: five questions (why the weight; what grew; V₂ from T; γ_P = 1/T₁; which has the larger γ_P).
