# Notes: Simple harmonic motion (সরল স্পন্দন গতি)

Book: physics-9-10 › 2.2 › p37 (second figure on the page; the first is পর্যায়বৃত্ত গতি)

## What the page shows
Topic ২.২, the part সরল স্পন্দন গতি (Simple Harmonic Motion). Picture চিত্র ২.০২: a girl on a swing, "দোলনা সরল
স্পন্দন গতির একটি উদাহরণ". The paragraph: simple harmonic motion is a special kind of periodic motion
(পর্যায়বৃত্তগতি); the body oscillates (স্পন্দিত হয়) on both sides of a fixed point, the equilibrium point
(সাম্যবিন্দু, equilibrium point); at both ends (প্রান্ত) of the path the speed (দ্রুতি) is zero; going from an end
toward the equilibrium point the speed rises step by step (ক্রমান্বয়ে বাড়তে থাকে), is greatest (সর্বোচ্চ দ্রুতি)
at the equilibrium point, then falls to zero at the other end; it turns, comes back the same way and stops for an
instant at the first end: that whole journey takes one period (এক পর্যায়কাল), and the pattern repeats. Examples:
a body hung from a spring (স্প্রিং), a child on a swing, a clock pendulum (ঘড়ির পেন্ডুলাম), air molecules carrying
sound. Screenshot: `reference/page-37.png`.

## The one idea to teach
The body swings to and fro about the সাম্যবিন্দু: its speed is zero at each end, grows toward the সাম্যবিন্দু where it
is greatest, then falls to zero at the other end; one full back-and-forth takes one পর্যায়কাল.

## Terms (as in the book)
সরল স্পন্দন গতি, পর্যায়বৃত্ত গতি, স্পন্দিত, সাম্যবিন্দু, প্রান্ত, দ্রুতি, শূন্য, সর্বোচ্চ দ্রুতি, ক্রমান্বয়ে বাড়ে/কমে,
পর্যায়কাল, দোলনা, স্প্রিং, ঘড়ির পেন্ডুলাম. Units in the panel: মি/সে (m/s) and সেকেন্ড.

## How it works
- 3D: a girl on a swing hung from a bamboo frame (two crossed pairs of poles tied with rope, the way village swings
  are often built), seen from the side as in চিত্র ২.০২. The poles splay wide (2 m each way), so that from the side
  no pole ever passes in front of the girl, the path, the dots or the arrow, even at 25°; the camera frames the
  swinging part (top pole, the arc, the labels under it) and lets the legs run off the edges. Her plait hangs down
  her back and trails a little when she swings forward. Second example (chips দোলনা | স্প্রিং): a 2 kg slotted mass
  on a spring on a laboratory stand, moving up and down (the base and the top of the rod are cropped).
- What makes the idea visible: an orange speed arrow on the moving body (its length is the speed; it vanishes at
  the ends), blue dots on the path where the body is every T/16 (a "strobe photo": close together near the ends,
  far apart in the middle; the dot it passes lights up), and labels pinned to the path: "প্রান্ত / দ্রুতি শূন্য" below
  and outside both ends, "সাম্যবিন্দু / দ্রুতি সর্বোচ্চ" under the middle.
- Panel: the speed now (মি/সে); a graph of the speed over one period ("দ্রুতি" up, "সময় →" along), with the stops
  প্রান্ত, সাম্যবিন্দু, প্রান্ত, সাম্যবিন্দু, প্রান্ত and a bracket "← এক পর্যায়কাল = ২.৪৬ সেকেন্ড →" under them, and a
  dot that runs along it in step with the swing; the idea in the book's words ("প্রান্তে দ্রুতি শূন্য, সাম্যবিন্দুতে
  সর্বোচ্চ।") and what the dots mean; buttons থামাও/চালাও, ধীরে (time 4× slower, said on the 3D view), the example
  chips.
- The student pulls the seat, a rope or the mass to one side and lets go: that becomes an end, and it oscillates
  from there (amplitude 8°–25° for the swing, starting at 24°; 6–15 cm for the spring, starting at 12 cm; let go
  nearer the middle and it is carried out to the smallest). The first-use hand points at the near rope high up,
  where it hardly moves, so its label "টেনে ছেড়ে দাও" falls in the open space under the top pole.
- Keys: space play/pause, ← → one dot (T/16) back/forward (pauses), ↑ ↓ bigger/smaller swing, s slow, 1/2 example,
  f/v views.
- Test (5 questions, tap answers, each one sets up the 3D and the moving body is shown slowed down, without the
  arrow, dots and labels until the answer):
  - which of the points ক/খ/গ (letters just under the swing's path) is fastest, or has zero speed (wrong: the other
    points, "all the same");
  - how the speed changes along a part of the path marked with purple dashes and an arrowhead; the question does
    not say which way that is, the student finds the সাম্যবিন্দু (wrong: the opposite, "the same", "rises then
    falls"); the worked answer says which way the arrow pointed;
  - which journey takes one পর্যায়কাল, ক → গ → ক (wrong: ক → গ, ক → খ, খ → গ → খ);
  - the period from the time from one end to the other, 2h, with real-life times (a swing 1–2 s, a mass on a spring
    0.4–1 s; wrong: h, h/2, 4h);
  - which everyday motion is simple harmonic: a clock pendulum or whichever of "a child on a swing" and "a mass on a
    spring" is not on the screen (wrong: clock hand and fan blade, which are periodic but go round, and a falling
    ball); the worked answer explains the wrong choice that was tapped.

## Physics
- x = x0 cos ωt from rest at an end, v = −x0 ω sin ωt, period T = 2π/ω.
- Swing: the ropes are 1.7 m long to the seat; a seated child's centre of mass is about 0.2 m above the seat, so the
  pendulum length is L = 1.5 m to the centre of mass: ω = √(g/L), T = 2.46 s. The seat (and the path, the dots, the
  arrow and the speed shown) moves on the 1.7 m circle. This is the small-swing formula: up to 25° the real
  pendulum period is at most 1.3 % longer (checked in `test/unit/simple-harmonic-motion.test.mjs`), so the figure
  keeps the swing within 25°. Greatest speed of the seat at 24°: 1.82 m/s.
- Spring: m = 2 kg, k = 50 N/m, ω = 5 rad/s, T = 1.26 s (exact); it hangs 0.392 m stretched (mg/k) at the
  সাম্যবিন্দু, so even at the top end (15 cm up) the spring is still stretched. Greatest speed at 12 cm: 0.60 m/s.
- The figure does not claim that the period is independent of the amplitude (the book does not say it here).
- The plait trailing behind is a touch of life (air pushes it back as she swings forward), not a claim.

## Open questions
- The child figure is simple and stylised (rounded shapes, no face detail beyond the eyes). Replace it, or remove
  it, if the owner finds it crude.
- Bamboo frame instead of the book's (unseen) support: chosen to look like a Bangladeshi swing; its legs splay wide
  so that they stay out of the way of the swinging girl.
- "মি/সে" for m/s: the book writes m/s in formulas; the Bangla abbreviation reads better in a Bangla panel.

## Spring graph update (v0.3.0)
- Keep the existing vertical spring, its 2 kg mass, 50 N/m stiffness and motion controls. Add a violet/plum
  background, mint coil, turquoise stand, coral clamp, cobalt base and gold masses.
- Motion view: signed displacement, velocity and acceleration over one period. Positive is UP from equilibrium;
  the gold/cyan/lilac curves, current numbers and cursors all use the same oscillator state as the mass.
- Energy view: gold kinetic K and coral potential U in an energy-share ring and time graph, with constant total E.
  Since this spring is vertical, U is the combined elastic PLUS gravitational potential relative to equilibrium,
  U = kx²/2; it is NOT elastic energy measured from the spring's natural length. The caption states the reference.
- Graph views occupy the same space, including while a hand holds the mass (then a release hint hides the graphs).
  The shared five phase buttons pause/inspect both views. The compact phone view prioritises the scene and charts;
  its period is written on the common time axis. The swing retains its previous speed-graph lesson.
